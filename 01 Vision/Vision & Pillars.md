# Vision & Pillars

## High Concept

> *A young girl lands in Oz and discovers the fairy tale is a machine. To get home, she must break it — with a pair of impossible shoes and three companions who each believe they are missing the one thing they need.*

A third-person action-adventure built on **mobility + summons**. Dorothy is not a warrior. She is fast, clever, and relentless — and she fights by borrowing the courage, heart, and brains of friends who don't believe they have any.

## Player Fantasy

You are **small in a large, hostile world**. You survive by movement, positioning, and choosing the right friend for the right fight. The power curve is not “Dorothy gets stronger” — it is **“Dorothy's circle gets whole.”**

## Design Pillars

### 1. Home is the horizon
Every system points home. The shoes, the companions, the quests, even the UI are all colored by the ache of *there's no place like home* (expressed in our own words, not the film's). The ending must feel earned, not given.

### 2. You cannot do this alone
Combat is built around **one summon at a time** with distinct, thematic kits. Companions are not stat sticks — they are answers to specific problems. The player's skill is *reading the fight and choosing the friend.*

### 3. Click your heels
The red shoes are the signature verb. They are simultaneously:
- a **dodge** (short blink, i-frames),
- a **traversal tool** (charged long teleport, gated upgrades),
- an **expression of mastery** (chained blinks, kick-launch combos).

If the shoes feel bad, the game feels bad. Protect this verb.

### 4. Oz is beautiful and wrong
A twisted storybook: candy-colored surfaces over rot, chewed-up landscapes, charming creatures with dead eyes. The contradiction *is* the art direction.

### 5. Small girl, big world
Scale is a mechanic and a mood. Mombi is a building. The Wizard is a façade. The house that kills the Witch of the East is a house. Camera and level design should constantly remind the player how small Dorothy is.

## Tone

Whimsical dread. Warm and funny in the quiet moments, genuinely unnerving in the dark ones. Never grimdark; always storybook.

## Reference Points

| Game | What we borrow |
| --- | --- |
| *Hades* | Combat clarity, readable telegraphs, run-based retry language |
| *Kena: Bridge of Spirits* | Charm, scale, companion warmth, production polish target |
| *The Legend of Zelda* | Ability-gated world progression, environmental puzzles |
| *Psychonauts* | Personality-forward levels, thematic set pieces |
| *It Takes Two* | Co-op energy — but solo, with AI companions |

## What Makes It Different

1. **Summon-as-loadout** with thematic meters (Courage / Heart / Brain) instead of a weapon wheel.
2. **Mobility-first heroine** — the dodge is also the fast-travel and the puzzle key.
3. **Diegetic basket inventory** and **Toto wayfinding** instead of menus and minimap arrows.
4. **Boss-per-companion** — every friend is earned by beating the thing that broke them.

## Non-Goals

- Not open world.
- Not a looter or RPG with stat grind.
- Not multiplayer (at launch).
- Not a horror game, despite the dread.
- Not a remake or adaptation of the 1939 film.
