# IP & Legal (Public Domain Oz)

> [!warning] Not legal advice
> This is a working checklist for design decisions. Get IP counsel before marketing or store submission.

## What Is Safe (Public Domain)

L. Frank Baum's **original Oz books** are public domain in the US, including:

- *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz* (1900) — Dorothy, Toto, Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, Cowardly Lion, the Witches, the Wizard, Emerald City, Munchkinland, silver shoes, flying monkeys, the poppy field.
- *The Marvelous Land of Oz* (1904) — **Mombi**, **Tip**, **Ozma**, Jack Pumpkinhead, the Powder of Life.
- Later Baum books (through *Glinda of Oz*, 1920) — Glinda, the quadrant geography, much of the wider cast.
- Denslow's 1900 illustrations are also public domain in the US.

**Rule:** Design from the **books**, not the film.

## What Is Risky (MGM / Warner Bros.)

The 1939 film introduced or popularized elements that remain protected or associated with the studio:

| Element | Risk | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ruby slippers** | MGM creation; strong trade-dress association | Use **silver shoes** (book canon) or an original design. See below. |
| **Green-skinned Wicked Witch** | MGM invention (book witch is not green) | Design an original witch look. |
| **“Over the Rainbow”** | Protected song | Never use or quote. Original score only. |
| **Film score / Munchkin songs** | Protected | Original music. |
| **Flying monkey film design** | MGM visual design | Original creature design. |
| **Glinda's gown/crown, Witch makeup, crystal ball** | Film iconography | Original designs. |
| **“There's no place like home”** | Short phrase (generally not copyrightable) but heavily associated | Paraphrase; don't use as marketing tagline without review. |
| **Film stills / actor likenesses** | Protected | Never use. |

## The Red Shoes Decision (Important)

The founder notes say **red shoes**. In Baum, the shoes are **silver**.

- **Silver shoes** = unambiguous public domain, canon-accurate.
- **Red shoes** = generically fine as a color, but *ruby slippers with bows and sequins* drift toward MGM trade dress.
- **Recommendation:** ship **silver** as canon, with an original silhouette (e.g., heeled glass-metal, no bow), and treat a red variant as a **post-legal-review cosmetic** only.
- Keep the mechanic name neutral: **“the Shoes”** internally, “Silver Shoes” in-world.

## Character Name Checks

- “Oz,” “Dorothy,” “Toto,” “Mombi,” “Ozma,” “Tip,” “Glinda,” “Locasta,” “Munchkin” — public domain character names.
- Avoid naming the project in a way that implies film affiliation (no “1939,” no “ruby,” no “rainbow”).
- Run a **trademark search** for the final title before announcing.

## Originality Guardrails

1. Original character designs, costumes, and creature silhouettes.
2. Original music and sound design.
3. Original dialogue; no film lines.
4. Book-first lore (silver shoes, Locasta in the North, Glinda in the South).
5. Document design provenance in the art bible ([[Art & Audio Direction]]).
